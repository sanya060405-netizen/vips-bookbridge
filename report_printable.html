Abstract

BookBridge is a web-based campus exchange directory that connects students who have unused learning materials with students who need them. Today, students frequently require textbooks, reference manuals, and practical guides for their academic courses, but purchasing brand-new retail copies each semester is expensive. At the same time, many senior and peer students possess textbooks and study materials they no longer actively use. These materials often sit unused in dormitories and homes, even though other students could benefit from borrowing, exchanging, or receiving them.

This application allows registered students to search, filter, and browse listings by book title, author, subject module, and course (such as MCA, B.Tech CS, BCA). The platform supports three flexible resource-sharing models: Lending (with a specified borrowing timeframe), Exchange (book-for-book trade), and Free Donation. Through an intuitive request-interest flow, students can communicate their requirements and propose handover arrangements. To protect student privacy, the system strictly avoids publishing personal residential addresses and coordinates physical handovers exclusively at approved, monitored university meeting points such as the Central Library Foyer and Student Activity Centre (SAC).

The project is built using HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript for the frontend, Python with the Flask web framework for the backend, and SQLite for relational database management. The web portal includes active search filtering, listing creation/editing, student dashboards for managing requests, and comprehensive campus handover guidelines. All automated test cases written for the project pass successfully.

Keywords: Campus Book Exchange, Peer-to-Peer Resource Sharing, Academic Lending, Flask Web Framework, SQLite, Web Development, Campus Safety.

1. Introduction

1.1 Background

Textbooks, syllabus reference books, and laboratory manuals represent an essential yet recurring financial cost for university students. In professional technical degrees like the Master of Computer Applications (MCA) and Computer Science Engineering, prescribed reference volumes—such as Database System Concepts by Korth, Computer Networks by Tanenbaum, and Data Structures by Cormen—command substantial retail prices. Once students conclude semester examinations, these books frequently remain unused, while incoming juniors seek identical copies for their coursework.

While digital PDFs are common, physical textbooks remain indispensable for sustained study, mathematical problem-solving, and university open-book evaluations. However, university campuses typically lack an organized, secure directory to facilitate direct peer-to-peer textbook reuse. Commercial buyback platforms impose heavy courier cuts, while informal messaging groups suffer from clutter and lack privacy protection.

1.2 Problem Statement

When a student needs a course textbook or has finished using one, finding a peer is not straightforward. Searching across unorganized student chat groups or generic classified websites is time-consuming, unindexed, and exposes students' personal phone numbers publicly. Commercial buyback portals offer negligible repurchase value and require shipping. Furthermore, meeting unknown individuals off-campus presents safety concerns. Therefore, there is a clear need for an easy, campus-centric web tool that catalogs available study materials, enables structured interest requests, and enforces safe handovers strictly at monitored university premises.

2. System Analysis

2.1 Existing Methods and Their Limitations

Currently, university students rely on informal, fragmented methods to procure second-hand textbooks, each with notable limitations:

  • Commercial Resale Portals: Sites like OLX or commercial resellers charge commission fees, involve shipping delays, and fail to cater to specific university course syllabi.
  • Unorganized Chat Groups: WhatsApp and Telegram batch groups are flooded with unstructured requests that quickly get buried without searchability or status tracking.
  • Privacy Exposure: Posting book requests in broad social media groups exposes personal phone numbers and identities publicly.
  • Physical Safety Risks: Informal trades often lead students to arrange unverified meetups at off-campus street locations or after hours.

4. System Design

4.1 System Architecture

The application follows a clean Model-View-Controller (MVC) architecture. The student interacts with the web browser, which sends HTTP requests to the Flask backend (app.py). Flask coordinates request validation, executes business logic, interacts with the SQLite database (database.py), and returns dynamically rendered templates.

Step 1 – User Browser (Frontend): Renders HTML5 templates styled with Bootstrap 5 and custom CSS. Dispatches GET filter queries and POST request submissions.

Step 2 – Flask Server (app.py): Handles URL routing, session authentication, input validation, flash alerts, and orchestrates exchange workflows.

Step 3 – Exchange Workflow Engine: Manages state transitions of listings (Available → Pending Handover → Completed) and handles interest communication between students.

Step 4 – SQLite Database (database.py): Enforces relational integrity across normalized tables and runs parameterized SQL queries.

5.2 (Continued) Request-Interest Workflow & State Transitions

The lifecycle of an exchange progresses across four clear stages:

  • Available: The material is published in the catalogue and open to all students.
  • Pending Review: A student expresses interest. An exchange_requests record is saved with status 'Pending'.
  • Pending Handover: The owner accepts the request and writes a meeting note. The book availability automatically updates to 'Pending Handover' to alert other browsing students.
  • Completed: After meeting at the approved campus spot and verifying the book, the owner clicks 'Confirm Book Handed Over', marking both records as 'Completed'.

5.3 Campus Safety, Privacy, and Handover Protocols

To eliminate physical safety risks and address privacy concerns, BookBridge enforces four strict protocols:

  • Zero Home Address Collection: The system never asks for or displays personal residential addresses or hostel room numbers.
  • Approved Campus Meeting Points: All exchanges must occur at pre-approved, CCTV-monitored university spots (Central Library Foyer, SAC Atrium, MCA Lab 102).
  • Daylight Operating Hours: Exchanges are scheduled between 8:30 AM and 6:00 PM during regular academic working hours.
  • On-Site Inspection Checklist: Students verify textbook syllabus, edition, and page condition in person before confirming handover completion.

8. Limitations, Future Scope, and Conclusion

8.1 Limitations

  • Local Campus Deployment: The prototype currently runs locally and requires deployment on university intranet servers for cross-device access across the campus network.
  • Manual Physical Inspection: Evaluation of physical wear relies on student descriptions until visual inspection takes place at the handover meeting point.
  • No Real-Time Push Notifications: Students check their web dashboard to see new incoming requests rather than receiving SMS or push notifications.
  • Single Campus Boundary: Handover spots are configured for a single university campus and do not support multi-city university branches.

8.2 Future Scope

  • Automated SMS & Email Alerts: Integrate college SMTP or SMS gateways to alert owners immediately when an interest request is posted.
  • Handover QR Verification: Generate unique QR codes scanned at the library desk to timestamp and record physical book exchanges.
  • ISBN Barcode Scanner: Enable mobile camera barcode scanning to auto-fetch book title, edition, and publisher metadata.
  • AI-Based Syllabus Matcher: Automatically recommend relevant senior textbooks based on enrolled courses during semester registration.

8.3 Conclusion

BookBridge fulfills all objectives established for this minor project. It provides university students with an affordable, sustainable, and privacy-conscious alternative to buying new commercial textbooks each semester. By combining a searchable web interface, an embedded relational database, a request-interest state machine, and approved on-campus handover protocols, the project provides a practical application of web development and database concepts taught in the MCA curriculum.
